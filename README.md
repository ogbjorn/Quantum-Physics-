    <h2>What is Quantum Physics?</h2>
    <p>
      Quantum physics is the branch of science that deals with the behavior of particles on the smallest scales: atoms and subatomic particles.
      Unlike regular physics (classical physics), quantum physics shows us that things can exist in multiple states at once, and outcomes can be
      unpredictable until they’re observed.
    </p>
  </section>

  <section>
    <h2>Key Concepts</h2>

    <h3>1. Superposition</h3>
    <p>
      Particles can exist in multiple states at once until observed. Think of it like flipping a coin — it’s both heads and tails in the air.
    </p>
    <div class="example">
      Example: An electron can be in two energy levels at the same time until it's measured.
    </div>

    <h3>2. Entanglement</h3>
    <p>
      Two particles can be linked so that the state of one instantly affects the other, even across large distances.
    </p>
    <div class="example">
      Example: If you entangle two particles and send one to the moon, changing the one on Earth will instantly affect the one on the moon.
    </div>

    <h3>3. Quantum Tunneling</h3>
    <p>
      A particle can pass through a barrier it technically shouldn’t be able to, thanks to probability and wave behavior.
    </p>
    <div class="example">
      Example: Electrons in a microchip sometimes “tunnel” through barriers, enabling modern electronics to work efficiently.
    </div>
  </section>

  <section>
    <h2>Why Does It Matter?</h2>
    <p>
      Quantum physics powers much of our modern world — from lasers and computers to MRI machines and quantum computers in development.
    </p>
  </section>

  <section>
    <h2>Fun Fact</h2>
    <p>
      The famous physicist Niels Bohr said: <em>"If quantum mechanics hasn't profoundly shocked you, you haven't understood it yet."</em>
